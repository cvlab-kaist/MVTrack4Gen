Abstract

Synthesizing a video from a novel camera viewpoint given a monocular reference video demands both geometric consistency and motion fidelity to the reference. Existing novel-view generation methods that rely on explicit 3D representations are bounded by the accuracy of off-the-shelf reconstruction modules, which produce inaccurate depth for dynamic objects in monocular videos, whereas pipelines that rely solely on diffusion priors lack geometric and motion consistency. In this work, we introduce multi-view tracking as an additional motion signal that supervises the diffusion model, enforcing both motion and geometric consistency between the reference and generated views. Our key finding is that, as an emergent property, at specific attention layers each query point highly attends to its corresponding point across views in the attention map. We exploit this structure by routing the features of these layers into an auxiliary cross-view tracking head, and jointly train a video diffusion model with a point-tracking objective that makes the model follow the reference motion and preserves geometric consistency without any explicit 3D reconstruction. On diverse benchmarks, our method achieves state-of-the-art geometric consistency and camera-pose accuracy without requiring depth estimation or 3D reconstruction at inference.
